## Research Papers & Sources (20)

1. IUCN (2012). IUCN Red List Categories and Criteria: Version 3.1 (2nd ed.). IUCN Species Survival Commission — Foundational global extinction-risk classification standard.
2. M. Shaffer (1981). Minimum Population Sizes for Species Conservation. BioScience — Foundational Minimum Viable Population theory.
3. R. Lande (1993). Risks of Population Extinction from Demographic and Environmental Stochasticity and Random Catastrophes. The American Naturalist — Foundational population-viability-analysis theoretical framework.
4. G. Caughley (1994). Directions in Conservation Biology. Journal of Animal Ecology — Foundational conservation-biology methodological framework distinguishing decline theory from small-population theory.
5. S. Possingham, S. Andelman, M. Burgman, R. Medellin, L. Master, D. Keith (2002). Limits to the Use of Threatened Species Lists. Trends in Ecology & Evolution — Critical review of Red List methodology and its practical limits.
6. H. Possingham, K. Wilson, S. Andelman, C. Vynne (2006). Species Distribution and Conservation Planning. In Principles of Conservation Biology — Standard applied conservation-planning methodology.
7. R. Frankham, J. Ballou, D. Briscoe (2010). Introduction to Conservation Genetics (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press — Foundational genetic-diversity/conservation-genetics reference.
8. P. Bunnell, C. Possingham, K. Wilson (2004). Prioritizing Conservation Investments for Threatened Species Using a Return-on-Investment Approach. Biological Conservation — Foundational conservation-triage cost-effectiveness framework.
9. H. Possingham, S. Andelman, B. Noon, S. Trombulak, H. Pulliam (2001). Making Smart Conservation Decisions. In Conservation Biology: Research Priorities for the Next Decade — Applied decision-theory framework for conservation strategy.
10. J. Hanski (1998). Metapopulation Dynamics. Nature — Foundational metapopulation/habitat-corridor theoretical framework.
11. E. O. Wilson (1992). The Diversity of Life. Harvard University Press — Foundational biodiversity-conservation reference synthesizing extinction-risk research.
12. S. Pimm, G. Russell, J. Gittleman, T. Brooks (1995). The Future of Biodiversity. Science — Landmark research on extinction-rate estimation and drivers.
13. IPBES (2019). Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services. Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services — Authoritative global biodiversity-decline evidence base.
14. D. Wilcove, D. Rothstein, J. Dubow, A. Phillips, E. Losos (1998). Quantifying Threats to Imperiled Species in the United States. BioScience — Empirical root-cause-of-decline classification research.
15. J. Rands, W. Adams, L. Bennun, S. Butchart, A. Clements, D. Coomes, et al. (2010). Biodiversity Conservation: Challenges Beyond 2010. Science — Comprehensive review of global conservation-strategy challenges.
16. M. Soulé (1985). What Is Conservation Biology? BioScience — Foundational conservation-biology disciplinary framework.
17. W. Ripple, C. Wolf, T. Newsome, et al. (2017). World Scientists' Warning to Humanity: A Second Notice. BioScience — Widely cited scientific consensus statement on biodiversity/extinction crisis drivers.
18. R. Redford, P. Coppolillo, E. Sanderson, et al. (2003). Mapping the Conservation Landscape. Conservation Biology — Applied landscape-scale conservation-strategy planning methodology.
19. D. Simberloff (1988). The Contribution of Population and Community Biology to Conservation Science. Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics — Foundational integration of population/community ecology into conservation strategy.
20. IUCN SSC (2013). Guidelines for Reintroductions and Other Conservation Translocations. IUCN Species Survival Commission — Standard applied methodology for captive-breeding/reintroduction conservation strategy.
